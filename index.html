Broker Statement Adjustment Worksheet

Step-by-step help for Montana interest & dividend adjustments from a consolidated 1099

Before you begin

Why this matters

Montana taxes some interest and dividend income differently than the federal return does. TaxSlayer starts with the federal treatment, so for four common situations you need to make a manual Montana adjustment. This tool walks you through each one — use only the steps that apply to your client's statement.

Look at the client's consolidated 1099 and answer these four questions

If you answered “no” to all four questions, no Montana adjustment is needed for this statement. Otherwise, work through each step that applies, in order. When you're done, the Summary tab collects everything you'll need to enter into TaxSlayer.

Tip: after finishing a client's worksheet, open the Summary tab and use Print summary — include the printout with your files for Quality Review.

1099-INT · Box 3 · applies only if Box 3 has an amount

Interest on U.S. Savings Bonds & Treasury Obligations

Interest on U.S. Savings Bonds and Treasury obligations is federally taxable but is not taxable to Montana. TaxSlayer treats it the same as federal unless you subtract it yourself, so this entire amount is a straight Montana subtraction — no fund-by-fund breakdown needed.

Montana subtraction amount $0.00
Where this goes in TaxSlayer

Federal Section → Income → Interest and Dividends → Interest or Dividend Income → Add Interest and Dividends → Interest Income, Form 1099-INT. Enter the federal amount as shown on the statement, then scroll down to “Amount of Interest on U.S. Savings Bonds and Treasury Obligations you want subtracted from your state return” and enter the amount above. Confirm the state selected is Montana.

1099-INT · Box 8 · applies only if Box 8 has an amount

Tax-Exempt Interest

Tax-exempt interest is generally interest from municipal bonds and U.S. territories. Interest from Montana (and U.S. territory) bonds stays exempt for Montana. Interest from non-Montana municipal bonds is taxable to Montana, even though it's exempt federally — that non-Montana portion is what you need to add back. Find the fund-by-fund state breakdown in the broker statement's supplemental pages.
